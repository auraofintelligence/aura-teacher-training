DAY 43 / MOVEMENT 05

Scientific method as a discovery practice

Turn a personally meaningful research idea into a replicable comparison that could change your preferred explanation.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Turn a personally meaningful research idea into a replicable comparison that could change your preferred explanation.

A rigorous question specifies what would be observed and which explanations the observation could distinguish. Decide the method before inspecting the result. A pilot tests whether the procedure works; it does not automatically establish the hypothesis. Meaningful experiences and unusual research directions deserve precise descriptions, repeatable comparisons and room for informative disagreement. Keep a record of interruptions and exclusions instead of silently removing inconvenient observations.

A worked example

A teacher wonders whether a visual sequence helps adults identify source errors more reliably than a spoken sequence. For an illustrative rehearsal, two synthetic groups each receive ten matching practice claims. One score is higher, but the visual group also receives twice as long. The observed difference cannot isolate the teaching format. Equalising time or recording it as a separate variable makes the next comparison more informative.

Make, test and revise

  1. Write a question, a preferred explanation and a plausible alternative. State which observable result would make you reconsider the preferred explanation.
  2. Specify the comparison, instructions, timing, measurement scale and planned analysis. Declare exclusions before collecting or viewing any practice results.
  3. Rehearse the measurement with a reference example. Record whether two observers apply the scoring rule consistently and revise ambiguous instructions.
  4. Exchange the protocol with another adult. Ask them to reproduce the procedure using synthetic data, then document every clarification they needed.

Teach it to another adult

Explain the distinction between an observation and an explanation through the unequal-time example. Invite the peer to suggest another source of the difference.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A dated experiment card with question, comparisons, variables, units and planned interpretation.
  • A measurement rehearsal showing scoring differences, reference checks and any revised instructions.
  • An independent walkthrough with unresolved questions, preserved exclusions and a next-test decision.

Try a changed condition

Apply the same design discipline to an intention-and-material research question. Identify its operational observation without treating personal conviction as measurement.

Reflect on the result

  • Could the comparison distinguish your proposed explanations?
  • Which decision was made after seeing the data?
  • What does the pilot establish about the method?

Adapt the encounter

Allow an oral protocol, tactile comparison cards or an accessible spreadsheet. A partner may record observations without taking over the learner's research decisions.

Take it further

Prepare a short replication note explaining how a second investigator could obtain a different result without either group acting dishonestly.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 43. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Scientific inquiry turns an interesting idea into a question that observations are able to inform. Today's objective is to design an experiment with a clear claim, method, comparison and route to replication. The learner chooses an idea from their own work, including an unusual theory or a personally meaningful experience they want to investigate rigorously.

The inquiry begins with a precise question. A material design might seek a particular optical, acoustic or thermal behaviour. A learning study might compare two teaching sequences. A mind over matter investigation might examine a declared intention under identified conditions. The learner records what result would support, challenge or change the proposed explanation.

The science studio produces an experiment card. It contains the question, hypothesis, variables, material or participant context, comparison condition, method, instruments, units, timing, planned analysis and replication instructions. Relevant source material and permissions accompany the card. The class examines which conditions need control and which belong in the context record.

The learner rehearses measurement before testing the hypothesis. They check a reference or calibration point, document instrument settings and identify the smallest change the method is intended to resolve. A pilot observation tests whether the collection process is workable. The record preserves unexpected results and interruptions so the next investigator understands what occurred.

Technical builders create data capture and analysis. Creative learners make the method understandable through a scene, diagram or demonstration. Philosophical learners examine the explanation and alternative possibilities. Teachers ask whether another adult receives enough information to repeat the method. A second group then attempts a small replication or protocol walkthrough.

The source work connects environmental feedback, open research, materials, embodied experience and long- horizon questions. The Direct Hardware learning cycle adds observe, model, challenge, choose, measure and invent. Today's method gives those ambitions a repeatable route to discovery. [S03, pp. 5, 7, 11; S12, pp. 19-20; Project Atlas: Aura Direct Hardware]

The life reflection asks how the learner responds when an observation surprises them. They identify a preferred theory, the reasons it attracts them and the result that would prompt a change. Teaching practice introduces the experiment through its question and lets a newcomer perform one measurement or review step. Evidence includes the experiment card, calibration record, pilot data, alternative explanations and replication feedback. The learner now has a constructive scientific practice for the frontier inquiries ahead.

The learner also plans how an informative result will be shared. A method summary, source data, interpretation and next question receive appropriate audiences and permissions, letting another investigator continue the inquiry from the actual evidence.

Source: S03 Big Picture Thinking; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
