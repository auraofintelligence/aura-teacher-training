DAY 36 / MOVEMENT 04

A living community twin

Build a source-linked community-twin slice that answers one practical question across physical, service, ecological, permission and rule layers.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Build a source-linked community-twin slice that answers one practical question across physical, service, ecological, permission and rule layers.

A community twin becomes useful through its relationship with a place and the people responsible for its information. The source offers public-record, scanning and calibrated-measurement projects as different entry points. They are not interchangeable evidence of a complete living twin. Choose one question, represent the necessary layers and name who can correct each. Personal records do not automatically become community data. A map of access conditions must also distinguish measured features from assumptions; an attractive scene does not certify accessibility or establish permission to use a site.

A worked example

Illustrative example: a fictional hub wants to explain how an adult reaches a shared computer. Its slice links the entrance and path, opening-time record, shade condition, booking contact and venue rule. A mock width is explicitly invented. When the usual path closes, the model shows the dependency but cannot certify the alternative route. It returns a question for the designated site steward.

Make, test and revise

  1. Write the visitor question and select only relevant features. Give every record a date, source, owner or correction route and a status such as observed, public-source, inferred or illustrative.
  2. Connect five layers in a simple table, map or local scene. Keep service information, environmental conditions, selected people and applicable permissions separately identifiable.
  3. Run an arrival scenario, then change a timetable or close a path. Trace the affected records and explain where the model lacks evidence to give a dependable answer.
  4. Invite a consenting adult to review one appropriate layer, or use an assigned fictional steward. Record their correction and demonstrate how it changes the scenario.

Teach it to another adult

Start a newcomer with the practical question. Let them follow one feature to its source and ask what evidence would make an uncertain detail dependable.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A five-layer slice with source dates, statuses and correction responsibilities.
  • An initial and changed-condition scenario with traceable dependencies.
  • One attributed correction or observation plan and its effect on the model.

Try a changed condition

Change the service from a computer station to a water-refill point. Have the peer identify which layers remain useful and which require new observations, sources or operational responsibilities.

Reflect on the result

  • Which layer currently depends on an unverified assumption?
  • Who can correct the service information when it changes?
  • What would the model need before describing the alternative route as usable?

Adapt the encounter

Provide a text route, tactile or paper map and narrated walkthrough. Keep source records available outside any 3D interface and support contributed corrections in more than one format.

Take it further

Compare a public-record source with a permissioned local observation. Record disagreement and a verification route rather than automatically preferring the newer or more detailed item.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 36. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

A community twin represents a place through geometry, ecology, people, services, culture and rules. Today's objective is to build a small, source-linked slice that answers a useful question. The learner begins with one service or site rather than trying to model an entire community in a single exercise.

The selected question might concern access to a learning space, the resources needed for a wellbeing hub, a local water system or the route to a community event. The learner gathers permissioned observations and relevant public sources. A scene or map locates the features, while linked records describe their meaning, date, source and responsibilities.

The twin studio connects five layers: physical layout, service function, ecological conditions, people and permissions, and applicable rules. A feature may link to a photograph, measurement, source record and open question. Technical builders create a small data model or scene. Creative learners make the place intelligible. Civic learners check the source and decision relationships.

The current Minjerribah projects provide different entry points. Living Twin offers a public-record prototype. The Scan Pipeline supports local capture and open-format assets. Second Island explores measurement, calibration and explicit source gaps. The learner selects the project relevant to the question and describes the actual contribution it supplies. Project Atlas: Minjerribah Living Twin, Aura Scan Pipeline, Second Island

The class runs a scenario in the slice. A person arrives with a particular access need, a service changes its timetable or a resource becomes unavailable. The model explains the resulting dependency and the source used. The learner records what the twin handles, what requires another observation and who would validate the interpretation in practice.

The life reflection asks how the place supports or limits the learner's own agency. A doorway, transport route, shared kitchen, computer or trusted person may make an important activity possible. The learner connects that resource with their personal Aura and a best-self scenario. The personal and environmental twins now meet through a specific lived relationship.

Teaching practice introduces the slice to a newcomer through the useful question. The person follows a feature to its source and identifies an improvement or missing detail. Evidence includes the layered twin, source dates, permissions, scenario result, calibration or observation record and teaching feedback. This is a first civic digital- twin capability with a clear purpose and a route for growth. Later planetary work will extend the same source and relationship discipline across larger scales.

A local contributor reviews one layer for meaning and accuracy. The learner records the person’s correction and its source, then shows how the revised detail changes the scenario. This gives the twin a practical relationship with the community it represents.
