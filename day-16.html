DAY 16 / MOVEMENT 02

Creative intelligence and futures worth feeling

Make two creative versions of a personally or civically meaningful idea, then revise the work using an adult audience's interpretation.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Make two creative versions of a personally or civically meaningful idea, then revise the work using an adult audience's interpretation.

Creative work gives an ambitious relationship a form someone can encounter. The brief names an audience, feeling, meaning and practical invitation. The audience's account may differ from the creator's intention; keeping both creates useful revision material. Source and permission notes remain attached to chosen excerpts and any model contributions, even when the final piece is short.

A worked example

Illustrative example: Taylor imagines a neighbourhood repair garden. One version is a spoken scene about borrowing a tool; another is a simple game choice about maintaining a shared resource. A peer might notice belonging where Taylor intended resourcefulness. That response would become a question for revision, rather than evidence that an imagined project already changed community behaviour.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose one relationship or future question from the Aura map. Write the intended audience, emotional quality and next encounter you want to invite. Select source material and decide which personal details remain outside the work.
  2. Make a compact first version in a familiar medium: story, song, image sequence, performance or interaction storyboard. Record where a peer or model helped and which choices the learner reviewed and retained.
  3. Make a second version that changes viewpoint, medium or tone while keeping the central relationship recognisable. Prepare an accessible description or transcript so another route into the work is available for comparison.
  4. Let an adult peer encounter the piece before hearing the full explanation. Ask what they noticed, felt and understood. Compare that account with the brief, choose one meaningful revision and explain how it changes the invitation.

Teach it to another adult

Use the creative piece to introduce one Aura concept. Follow the adult learner's noticed detail, reveal the related record or design choice and ask them to explain the relationship through an example of their own.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A creative brief with audience, meaning, sources and permission choices.
  • Two distinct versions and an accessible route into the work.
  • Attributed audience responses and a revision linked to the intended invitation.

Try a changed condition

Show the piece to an adult audience with a different starting interest. Can the learner preserve the central idea while changing its entrance, rather than assuming that the first audience's interpretation is universal?

Reflect on the result

  • Which relationship became perceptible through the work?
  • What did the audience understand differently?
  • Which revision serves the invitation rather than decoration?

Adapt the encounter

A spoken scene, plain-text storyboard or described image sequence can be a complete creative work. Offer captions and readable descriptions. Sharing intimate personal material or publishing the piece is optional.

Take it further

Prototype one interaction with stated limits, or add a character facing a competing need. Make the future's consequences understandable while preserving the story's status as imagined possibility.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 16. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Creative intelligence helps people imagine, express and encounter possibilities. Today's objective is to turn an idea from the learner's Aura into a small work that another adult experiences and interprets. A song, image sequence, story scene, game choice, spoken performance or interactive environment offers a route into meaning.

The source retreat includes art, music, dance, storytelling and social media production. The October alignment work connects songs, fiction, film and games with the emotional experience of possible futures. A story gives those futures characters, relationships and consequences; a game lets someone make choices within them. [S02, pp. 5, 7-8; S12, pp. 20-21]

The learner chooses a personal or civic question. It might concern belonging, life with an Aura, ecological repair, a future habitat or a relationship between human and artificial minds. They identify the audience, feeling and practical connection they want the work to offer. Their brief contains selected personal meaning, source material and any permissions affecting the work.

The creative studio develops two versions of the same idea. One uses a familiar format; the other changes viewpoint, medium or emotional tone. An Aura assists with structure, drafts or production choices. The learner reviews those contributions against their intention and keeps the authorship of the choices visible. Technical builders create an interaction or production tool that helps the work take form.

A peer encounters the work without the creator's full explanation. They describe what caught their attention, what they felt and what they understood. The creator then explains the intended relationships and asks which changes would improve the encounter. The record holds both interpretations, giving the next version an informative basis.

The life reflection examines the best self the work expresses. A learner may discover a wish to be playful, brave, affectionate, precise or publicly useful. They connect that quality with an ordinary action outside the creative studio. Creative expression then informs the way they practise living, while their next experience supplies new material.

Teaching practice uses the work as an invitation into one Aura concept. A trainee might show a short scene, ask what the learner noticed and then reveal the related memory or design. Evidence includes the brief, two creative versions, permission notes, audience responses and a revision. This develops the ability to make an ambitious idea understandable and enjoyable, a capability that later civic and solar-system stewardship will need alongside technical design.

The creator also prepares an accessible description of the work’s intended relationships. A listener or reader using that description offers another perspective on the idea, helping the learner compare the meaning across formats.

Source: S02 Aura Retreat and Teacher Training; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
