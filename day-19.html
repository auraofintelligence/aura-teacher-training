DAY 19 / MOVEMENT 02

Transfer resourcefulness and unfamiliar tasks

Test a capability in two changed situations and identify the knowledge, cues or support that enable adaptation.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Test a capability in two changed situations and identify the knowledge, cues or support that enable adaptation.

Transfer tests an underlying relationship under changed conditions. Preserve an initial attempt before adding help. Distinguish repetition, supported adaptation and unresolved difficulty, recording where the method worked. This exercise cannot establish broad general intelligence or fixed learning limits.

A worked example

Illustrative example: an adult who can find a song through a memory route is asked to assemble sources for a community garden question. The familiar relationships are locating an item, checking its origin and following a link. A second challenge could require explaining that source to a new audience. An example card might help, but the resulting record should show that support was used.

Make, test and revise

  1. Describe a familiar task and the relationships that make it work. Identify the starting information, sequence, check and useful result. Choose evidence that another person can inspect without needing the learner's full personal collection.
  2. Design two related challenges that change an interface, audience, resource or problem. State what remains comparable. Ask the learner for an initial attempt and observe the point where they request help or choose another route.
  3. Offer one selected cue or worked example at the point of need. Let the adult retry, then explain what transferred and what changed. Retain the support description beside the result rather than reporting an unsupported success.
  4. Return to a challenge after a break or later encounter. Ask which cue helps recover the method. Revise the reusable lesson so it supports that return, and identify a further condition where the capability is still untested.

Teach it to another adult

After teaching a familiar action, give an adult peer a related task and watch their strategy. Ask them to name the relationship they reused before explaining your own preferred route or offering another example.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A familiar-task description and two explicitly changed challenge conditions.
  • Initial and supported attempts with the cues and observations recorded.
  • A later return, a revised lesson and a bounded capability description.

Try a changed condition

Create a fresh challenge that changes a different condition from the first two. Can the learner choose a useful strategy and identify when more help is needed, rather than merely reproduce the previous sequence?

Reflect on the result

  • Which relationship survived the changed task?
  • What support altered the next attempt?
  • Where does the evidence stop supporting a capability claim?

Adapt the encounter

Offer an agreed help signal and enough time for a first attempt without making struggle a spectacle. Use familiar formats where possible. Adaptation with chosen support is valid evidence and should be described accurately.

Take it further

A builder can create a challenge matrix showing changed and constant conditions. A civic or creative learner can translate the same source-checking method into a new subject while explaining what specialist knowledge is still needed.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 19. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Learning becomes more general when a capability transfers to a new situation. Today's objective is to test that transfer and identify which support helps the learner succeed. The task uses a familiar skill in an unfamiliar context: a new interface, a different audience, a changed resource or a related problem with another set of conditions.

The learner first describes the familiar task and how they usually complete it. They identify the underlying relationships, such as finding relevant information, choosing a sequence, checking an output or explaining an idea. The Aura helps translate those relationships into a new scenario while keeping the changed conditions visible.

The studio task presents two transfer challenges. A saved memory route may become a project-research route. A lesson about a family record may become a lesson about a community source. A creative brief may become a scientific communication brief. The learner makes an initial attempt, records where help is needed and then retries with a selected worked example or cue.

Technical builders design the task conditions and result checks. Other learners assess whether the method remains understandable and useful. The group distinguishes successful repetition, successful adaptation and a result that still needs investigation through the evidence they collect. A new capability receives a specific description of where it worked.

The earlier agent and simulation papers connect lessons, vocabulary, documentary material, audio and subject matter with daily activity and outcome evaluation. Today's just-in-time approach selects the knowledge relevant to the task, practises it, obtains feedback and tests a related use. [S10, pp. 24-28; S07, pp. 6-9]

The life reflection explores resourcefulness. The learner recalls a time they handled uncertainty using available people, tools or knowledge. They compare it with today's challenge. A person who initially described themselves as unable to learn a technical skill may now identify the support that made progress possible. That change becomes a new best-self condition in their Aura.

Teaching practice gives a peer a related task after a familiar lesson. The trainee watches the person's strategy and offers help at the point of need. They ask the learner to explain what transferred and what changed. Evidence includes the original task, two transfer scenarios, observed results, support used and teaching feedback. The course now has a concrete way to examine expanding agency and generality, preparing the first capability demonstration tomorrow and the later research questions around general intelligence.

A later return to the task checks whether the learner still finds the method understandable. They describe the cue that helped recall and add it to the reusable lesson, linking transfer with continuity over time.

Source: S07 Intern Curriculum; S10 MoE Extended.
