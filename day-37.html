DAY 37 / MOVEMENT 04

Contribution reciprocity and the C hour

Create a correction-ready C-Hour record that separates voluntary human time, paid activity, learning, resources and observed outcomes.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Create a correction-ready C-Hour record that separates voluntary human time, paid activity, learning, resources and observed outcomes.

One C-Hour records one verified hour of voluntary human contribution. It has no monetary value or conversion rate. A contribution can benefit several projects while the person’s time is counted once. The source’s braided-economy approach keeps financial arrangements and shared resources in their own records, alongside rather than inside the hour total. Verification concerns the specified contribution and its evidence; it does not certify every claimed social benefit. A community chooses recognition or invitations with actual conditions and availability. Essential care and belonging do not depend on accumulating hours.

A worked example

Illustrative example: Lee voluntarily spends forty-five minutes improving a welcome map, followed by thirty minutes learning a new layout tool for personal development. The contribution may be recorded as 0.75 C-Hour after verification; the learning session has its own description. Two projects can link to the same contribution identifier. A donated laptop and any printing cost remain separate resources, not additional human hours.

Make, test and revise

  1. Agree a task with a willing host or use a fictional rehearsal. Record purpose, voluntary status, support, intended audience, expected work and who can verify the activity.
  2. Prepare a proposed contribution record with person, activity, date, duration, evidence, verifier and status. Separate learning, paid work and resource use before adding a total.
  3. Rehearse verification against selected evidence. Link a second benefiting project to the existing identifier and check that the person’s contribution is not counted again.
  4. Correct an entered duration or mistaken outcome attribution. Retain the prior value, reason, author and date, then let the contributor choose a private acknowledgement or public project summary.

Teach it to another adult

Ask another adult to explain proposed versus verified status and why one useful outcome can involve several different records. Check that they do not describe hours as money.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A task brief identifying voluntary contribution and the responsible verifier.
  • A verified or clearly simulated record with one-time counting demonstrated.
  • A correction history and separate resource, learning and outcome records.

Try a changed condition

Introduce a paid teaching session followed by ten voluntary minutes helping the host pack away. Ask the learner to distinguish the arrangements and request clarification if voluntariness is uncertain.

Reflect on the result

  • What evidence supports this duration and activity?
  • Was one person’s time counted more than once?
  • Which claimed benefit needs evidence beyond the contribution record?

Adapt the encounter

Use a spoken activity account, assisted timing or a simple paper form. Verification can remain pending while a correction or audience choice is resolved.

Take it further

Draft a community invitation such as workshop access. State who offers it, actual availability and conditions without inventing a universal exchange price or entitlement.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 37. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Community capability grows through willing contributions, paid work, learning, shared resources and chosen reciprocity. Today's objective is to record these activities clearly and make a useful voluntary contribution understandable. The learner chooses a task welcomed by a community host and defines the outcome before beginning.

One C-hour records one verified hour of voluntary human contribution. The record carries the person, activity, date, duration, evidence or verifier, audience and correction history. One person's time is counted once even when several projects benefit. Communities choose recognition, thanks and reciprocal opportunities. Financial activity uses its own accounts. [S12, p. 16; S16, p. 10; Project Atlas: C-Hour Introduction]

The task may be helping a consenting adult use a service map, preparing a community-science observation, improving an accessible lesson, contributing to a shared creative work or repairing a source-linked twin record. The host explains the purpose and the support available. The learner's Aura helps prepare the task and capture the selected evidence afterwards.

The contribution studio creates a proposed record before the activity and a verified record afterwards. A forty- five-minute contribution and a thirty-minute learning session receive their own descriptions and durations. A second project links to the same contribution record where it benefited from the work. The learner checks identity, timing, audience and the result with the verifier.

The group examines a correction scenario. A duration was entered incorrectly or an outcome was attributed to the wrong activity. The record is revised with a reason, author and date. A public summary chosen by the contributor describes what improved and what remains to do. Private personal reflections retain their chosen audience.

The life reflection considers what the learner enjoys giving and what kinds of reciprocity feel meaningful. A thank- you, a shared meal, a learning invitation or a chance to collaborate may each matter differently. The learner identifies a contribution connected to their best self and a resource that would help them continue participating.

Teaching practice introduces the record to another adult through their own chosen activity. The trainee explains proposed and verified states, one-time counting and the distinction between learning and voluntary contribution. Evidence includes the task brief, completed work, corrected contribution record, verification and teaching feedback. The capability developed today supports the braided economy: useful work, financial arrangements, voluntary time and observed outcomes remain readable together, giving a community a fuller picture of what its people are making possible.

The contributor chooses the audience for their verified record. A private acknowledgement and a public project summary may serve different purposes. The learner records those choices and shows how recognition follows the community’s relationships and the person’s preferences.

Source: S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026; S16 Queens Planning 23 September 2026.
