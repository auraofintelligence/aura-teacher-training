DAY 35 / MOVEMENT 04

Culture place and the rules that apply

Trace a place-based proposal through dated sources, cultural relationships and the people authorised to make relevant decisions.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Trace a place-based proposal through dated sources, cultural relationships and the people authorised to make relevant decisions.

A useful place brief distinguishes law, venue policy, cultural permission and personal preference. They may affect the same action but have different sources and decision holders. The source asks learners to find current primary material and preserve its date and jurisdiction. Today’s example is illustrative, not legal advice. If a learner investigates a real project, they need the actual locality, operative source and relevant authority checked before relying on the result. Cultural authority cannot be inferred from appearance, residence or an AI-generated account; an unanswered permission question remains visible.

A worked example

Illustrative example: a fictional group wants to record an outdoor storytelling event. The practice brief contains a mock venue rule dated for the exercise and clearly labelled as invented. Recording participants, using a particular story and publishing a finished piece are separate questions. A designated venue contact cannot automatically grant cultural or individual recording permission. No exercise card is presented as an Australian legal requirement.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose a fictional event or an authorised real research question. Name place, proposed action, affected people and whether each supplied rule is a hypothetical or a dated primary source.
  2. Create rule cards for jurisdiction, operative period, scope, decision holder and review date. Keep venue conditions distinct from laws and flag any source whose current operation is unresolved.
  3. Map cultural and recording permissions without inventing authority. Record the question to ask, the appropriate route to identify an authorised contributor and the audience permitted for any supplied account.
  4. Walk another adult through one dependency from proposed action to decision. Change the event date or recording purpose and identify which source or permission must be reviewed.

Teach it to another adult

Explain one rule card in ordinary language and ask the peer to locate its source and responsible decision-maker. Stop before turning an unresolved research question into confident advice.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A place brief distinguishing hypothetical facts from checked sources.
  • Rule and permission cards with dates, scopes and unresolved authority questions.
  • A dependency walkthrough showing what changes when the proposed use changes.

Try a changed condition

Move the scenario to another locality. Ask the learner to identify what cannot be carried over and how they would find the relevant primary source or responsible contact.

Reflect on the result

  • Is this requirement a law, policy, permission or preference?
  • What justifies the claimed authority over the cultural material?
  • Which change would make the existing source card insufficient?

Adapt the encounter

Offer a single-action brief, plain-language source excerpts and a spoken dependency map. Keep complete citations available without making dense legal reading the only participation route.

Take it further

Prepare a source-update note showing who checks a later change and when. Distinguish finding a newer page from verifying that a particular rule now applies.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 35. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Civic stewardship joins personal understanding with the culture, law and relationships of a place. Today's objective is to trace one proposed action through the actual people and rules that shape it. The learner chooses a local example, such as a public event, a shared service, a land-use idea or a community recording project.

The place brief identifies locality, local government area, relevant jurisdictions, people affected, cultural relationships and the decision under consideration. The learner finds current primary sources for the selected rule question and records the source date. A rule card contains jurisdiction, operative period, territorial scope, affected action, responsible decision holder and a review date.

The civic studio connects the rule cards with the place model. A proposed event may involve venue permission, access, cultural material, noise, equipment and public communication. The learner follows one actual dependency and identifies the next person or source needed. A model assists with organising the material; the sources and responsible parties remain visible in the brief.

The source retreat includes laws, norms, languages, storytelling and culture. AuraOZ places cultural expectations within the personal model and later introduces participatory governance. The current civic projects add source- backed jurisdictional interfaces and community twins. Today's lesson connects those strands in a practical decision. [S02, p. 7; S04, introduction and Day 10; Project Atlas: P4A Xyz Cinema]

Cultural knowledge retains its contributors and agreed audience. A local invitation may permit a particular story, recording or teaching encounter. The learner records that scope and the person or group responsible for further decisions. A personally meaningful cultural account and a public source have their own places in the model, making the relationship understandable to future collaborators.

The reflection considers how the learner's own culture and legal surroundings influence action. A familiar practice may carry an assumption that differs elsewhere. They compare a remembered situation with the current place brief, then identify what a respectful newcomer would ask before participating.

Teaching practice gives another adult a simple route through the selected action and its rule cards. The trainee explains one dependency at a time and invites the person's question. Evidence includes the place brief, primary- source cards, cultural permissions, dependency map and unresolved source questions. The capability built today prepares community and solar-system stewardship: knowing the setting, recognising decision rights, finding the relevant rule and preserving the people whose knowledge makes responsible action possible.

The learner also checks a rule’s review date and identifies the route for verifying a later change. A source-led update practice keeps the twin useful as the place’s decisions and conditions evolve.

Source: S02 Aura Retreat and Teacher Training; S04 AuraOZ Learning Progression and Thought Experiment 2017.
