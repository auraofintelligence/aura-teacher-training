DAY 57 / MOVEMENT 06

Integrating evidence and the development portfolio

Build a portable portfolio in which another reviewer can trace a capability claim to evidence, context and a revision.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Build a portable portfolio in which another reviewer can trace a capability claim to evidence, context and a revision.

A portfolio is an argument about development, supported by artefacts rather than a catalogue of impressive topics. Select results that show recognition, transfer, inquiry, civic judgement and adult teaching. Preserve the conditions under which each result worked and the support it required. A failed or revised attempt can demonstrate valuable learning when its evidence and interpretation are clear. Protect private material through a deliberately selected review edition.

A worked example

A fictional learner titles a folder general intelligence achieved, but its contents show one successful scheduling exercise. The artefact is useful; the title overstates its scope. A revised claim identifies adaptation to one changed resource condition, with a baseline and unresolved failure. Another entry documents how a peer's correction changed a memory interpretation. Together they show different capabilities without pretending that one score describes the entire learner or Aura.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose a small set of results spanning the course. Give each a purpose, capability claim, source, version, audience and explanation of its significance.
  2. Pair a strong result with a difficult or revised one. State what each supports, which conditions matter and what further evidence would change your interpretation.
  3. Ask a peer to reproduce one small result or reconstruct one decision using only the pack. Record gaps before supplying extra explanation.
  4. Create a portable review edition and a recovery inventory. Preserve private records separately, then revise the tour in response to the peer's actual questions.

Teach it to another adult

Begin the portfolio tour with another adult's interest. Let them choose one result, follow its evidence and explain a next question in their own words.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A portfolio index mapping specific capability claims to sources, artefacts, context and permissions.
  • A strong-and-difficult result comparison retaining limitations, support and informative revisions.
  • A peer reconstruction record and a recoverable review edition with a named checkpoint date.

Try a changed condition

Give the portfolio to a reviewer unfamiliar with the course. Check whether they can distinguish original sources, your new work and a future proposal.

Reflect on the result

  • Which claim exceeds its supporting artefact?
  • What did the difficult result teach you?
  • Can the review edition travel without private material?

Adapt the encounter

Offer an accessible index, narrated tour or large-print evidence cards. A concise explanation may link to detailed technical material without hiding its relevant conditions.

Take it further

Write a scope statement for the next practicum describing demonstrated teaching capability and support welcomed, without presenting the portfolio as professional accreditation.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 57. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

A development portfolio shows what the learner and Aura have actually made, tested, revised and taught. Today's objective is to organise that evidence into a coherent account that another reviewer is able to follow. The learner chooses their strongest results and the unresolved questions that most deserve continuing work.

The portfolio follows the course's growth: personal map and lenses; avatar and interfaces; memory and sources; models and training; functions and agents; simulations and transfer; socialisation and civic judgement; infrastructure and science; planetary and solar-system design. Each result carries its purpose, source, version, permissions, context and evidence. A concise explanation links it to the learner's developing capability.

The review studio uses a rubric with several distinct areas. Personal recognition asks whether the Aura preserves the learner's meaning. Transfer asks how a method worked in a new condition. Scientific inquiry asks whether the method and observations are repeatable. Civic judgement asks whose interests and decisions appear. Teaching asks whether another adult learned and chose a next step. Stewardship asks how the work connects with its larger consequences.

The learner reviews a result they are proud of and a result that still needs work. They identify what the evidence supports, which conditions apply and what further test would be informative. A peer follows the source trail and attempts a small reproduction or walkthrough. Corrections improve the portfolio and the underlying artefact.

Technical builders prepare portable records and a recoverable copy. Creative learners shape an accessible narrative. Civic learners identify the permissions and responsibility attached to future use. The portfolio retains private and secret material within its selected scope; the public version contains the learner's chosen expressions and reviewed excerpts.

The source lineage is made readable within the pack. The 2017 AuraOZ file contributes the reflective architecture and learning sequence. The 2023-2024 exported XR and agent archives contribute addresses, sequences, functions and observers. September and October 2026 work contributes current alignment, civic systems and project connections. Each source is cited by its filename, section or page and the date it actually states. [S04; S08, pp. 29, 36-39; S09, pp. 20-24; S10, pp. 28-32; S12; S16]

Teaching practice is a portfolio tour for a newcomer, beginning with an interest they share. The person follows one result to its evidence and explains a next question. The learner records the feedback and improves the tour. Today's outcome is a grounded account of expanding agency and intelligence, ready for the demanding teaching practicum and public expression that follow.

The portfolio records its reviewer and date so future development begins with the relevant context. A later revision links back to this checkpoint, making growth through the programme visible and available for further inquiry.
