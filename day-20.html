DAY 20 / MOVEMENT 02

A first integrated capability demonstration

Integrate the first twenty days into a personally meaningful demonstration with traceable sources, correction, transfer, adult teaching and a usable handover.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Integrate the first twenty days into a personally meaningful demonstration with traceable sources, correction, transfer, adult teaching and a usable handover.

Follow one question through a personal map, selected records, an exchange, review and a useful result. Show how the parts serve the intention, what support was needed and what another adult can continue. These records provide a baseline for later tools and simulations.

A worked example

Illustrative example: fictional records support a music-and-repair gathering. The learner could retrieve an equipment note, compare plans and correct an assumed availability. A newcomer might prepare a task card. This remains a rehearsal until people, resources and arrangements are confirmed, rather than an announced operating event.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose one life, creative or civic question and write purpose, intended result, audience, records and permissions. Identify the capabilities involved and acceptance evidence. Ask a peer to review whether the scope is manageable before building the demonstration.
  2. Start at the map, follow relevant records and show a selected exchange leading to an artefact or next action. Explain one source and one correction. Distinguish draft, reviewed record, simulation and proposed external action throughout the route.
  3. Introduce a changed condition and ask a newcomer to perform one element with guidance, then a related example. Record their explanation, the support used and any unresolved difficulty. Do not substitute a polished presentation for their attempt.
  4. Package the brief, route, source trail, result, transfer evidence and revised plan for continuation. Ask a peer to perform one next step using only the handover, then add the context they actually needed.

Teach it to another adult

Have an adult newcomer explain how the result follows from the starting question and then carry out a small related action. Invite them to identify a source, a correction and a next choice in their own words.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A reviewed brief and coherent route through records, exchange and artefact.
  • A source check, visible correction and changed-condition teaching result.
  • A peer-tested continuation pack with support needs and revised next steps.

Try a changed condition

Change the audience, resource or intended outcome after the demonstration. Ask which records, assumptions and permissions must be reconsidered. Compare the revised route with the original rather than claiming universal capability from one successful encounter.

Reflect on the result

  • What expanded capability does the evidence actually show?
  • Which part still depends on particular support?
  • Can another person continue without hidden context?

Adapt the encounter

Offer several short encounters instead of one long presentation. A narrated portfolio, simple document or storyboard can demonstrate the relationships. Use sample records and the learner's chosen pacing; certification or clinical activity is outside this checkpoint.

Take it further

Identify a future function or specialist role that could improve the route. Describe its desired input and result for the tools movement, without claiming that it is implemented.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 20. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

The first twenty days have built personal meaning, a navigable map, an avatar, memory routes, learning examples, reasoning and teaching practice. Today's objective is to combine these into one coherent encounter. The learner chooses a question that matters in their life and demonstrates how their Aura helps them work with it.

The question may concern a creative project, an important relationship, a learning direction or a civic interest. The learner prepares a compact brief: purpose, relevant records, desired result, audience and permissions. They identify which capabilities are involved and what evidence would show the result is useful. A peer reviews the brief before the demonstration.

The demonstration starts at the personal map, follows relevant records, uses a selected model exchange and produces an artefact or next action. The learner explains a source, identifies a correction and shows a reusable route back to the work. A changed-condition example then tests whether the method transfers. The whole encounter follows the learner's chosen pacing and format.

Technical builders demonstrate the underlying exchange among records, retrieval, model and saved result. Creative participants demonstrate a story, performance or designed experience. Civic participants demonstrate a decision or communication grounded in sources. Each route makes its purpose and evidence visible. The group compares how the capabilities complement one another.

The reviewer asks whether the result matches the learner's intention, whether sources and corrections are traceable, whether permissions were respected and whether the learner is able to continue independently. They record the observations used for each judgement. A difficulty becomes a revision task with a named owner and a practical next step.

The reflection returns to the super-people invitation. The learner identifies an expanded capability: understanding a complex idea, expressing a desire, learning a tool, making something, teaching a peer or seeking support. They also identify the conditions that made it possible. The result enters the Aura as a specific experience of growing agency.

Teaching practice is the final part of the demonstration. A newcomer performs one element with the trainee's guidance and then tries a related example. The evidence pack contains the brief, source route, model exchange, artefact, transfer test, teaching feedback and revised plan. The next movement will turn personally meaningful capabilities into defined tools, specialist agents and simulations. The twenty-day checkpoint supplies a baseline against which that increased complexity will be compared. [S04, Days 7, 13-14; S12, pp. 7, 23-24]

The learner gives the demonstration pack to a peer for continuation. The peer performs one next step and reports any missing context. That result tests whether the capability leaves usable work behind for another collaborator.

Source: S04 AuraOZ Learning Progression and Thought Experiment 2017; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
