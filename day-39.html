DAY 39 / MOVEMENT 04

Teaching adults across different conditions

Adapt one demanding adult lesson across three learner contexts while preserving its meaning, independent choice and observable transfer.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Adapt one demanding adult lesson across three learner contexts while preserving its meaning, independent choice and observable transfer.

Adaptation changes the route into a concept, not the learner’s worth or the standard of honesty about what they understood. Start with their interest, existing knowledge and desired result. The source’s casual, normal and fast pacing concerns tempo and density; access support also includes language, processing time, captions, sensory conditions and assistance. A trainee should distinguish a teaching demonstration from professional qualification. Completing this adult studio does not confer clinical registration, recognised training authority or a licence to train children. The portfolio states the particular capability observed and where support remains useful.

A worked example

Illustrative example: the chosen concept is tracing a map feature to its source. One adult prefers a narrated journey, another an annotated paper map and a builder wants to inspect a small data table. All three should be able to identify the feature’s source, date and unresolved gap. Their formats differ, while the central relationship between a claim and its evidence remains intact.

Make, test and revise

  1. Write three context plans identifying interest, prior knowledge, chosen format, pace and access requirements. Set the same central understanding and an appropriate practical result for each.
  2. Prepare an overview, worked example, practice task, transfer task and recovery step. Use fictional or permissioned material and explain what will be observed before the peer agrees.
  3. Teach one version to a consenting adult. Record their artefact, questions, assistance and own account while an observer notes the sequence without inferring hidden comprehension.
  4. Revise one teaching element and repeat a related task. Provide a saved state, recap or visible example if the learner loses the route, then check whether they can continue with less prompting.

Teach it to another adult

Have the learner teach the relationship back using their own example. Ask what they would check in a different case rather than rewarding fluent repetition alone.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • Three context plans preserving one clearly stated concept.
  • A consented adult teaching record with artefact, assistance and learner feedback.
  • A transfer result and one documented revision or recovery step.

Try a changed condition

Provide a new feature and unfamiliar source format. Observe whether the learner finds the source, date and uncertainty without copying the original example’s answer.

Reflect on the result

  • Was the difficulty in the concept, explanation, interface or conditions?
  • Which assistance supported agency rather than taking over the task?
  • What precise teaching capability does this portfolio demonstrate?

Adapt the encounter

Let the adult select format and pace, decline personal examples and request support. Screen, tactile, audio and XR options remain choices rather than compulsory stages.

Take it further

Swap teacher and observer roles. Compare which skipped assumption each notices, then create a short prompt that helps future teachers pause and check that relationship.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 39. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

An Aura teacher works with adults whose resources, interests, communication and experience differ. Today's objective is to adapt a demanding concept without losing its meaning. The trainee chooses a lesson from the course and prepares it for three distinct learner contexts, then tests one version with a consenting peer.

The concept may be a function definition, source tracing, a community-twin layer or a three-observer simulation. The trainee begins with each person's interest and the result they want. They identify the information already familiar, the next unfamiliar relationship and the format the person prefers. A screen, voice, tactile demonstration, diagram, story and XR offer selectable forms.

The teaching studio creates a lesson with an accessible overview, a worked example, a practical task, a related transfer task and a route back to help. Casual, normal and fast pacing change the tempo and density. Additional support adjusts processing time, language, captions, visual complexity, sensory conditions and assisted interaction. The task's purpose remains recognisable across versions.

The volunteer curriculum contributes interactive workshops, role-play, real scenarios, mentorship, presentation and feedback. The current alignment paper adds progressive discovery and a plain introduction that starts with something the person cares about. These sources support the lesson design. [S07, pp. 6-11; S12, pp. 22-23]

A peer takes the lesson and explains what helped them understand. An observer notes the teaching sequence, questions and practical result. The trainee compares the observations with the person's account. They identify whether a difficulty arose from the task, the explanation, the interface or the conditions, then revise one element and test it again.

The life reflection asks how the trainee's own habits affect their teaching. A person fluent in a subject may skip the relationship a newcomer needs. A confident performer may fill pauses that another learner uses to think. The trainee records a specific practice they want to develop, such as asking a question before adding detail or letting a learner repeat an action independently.

Evidence for today includes the three context plans, selected lesson pack, observed encounter, learner artefact, transfer task and revision. The teaching scope describes the capability demonstrated and the support still useful. This prepares later public and civic teaching: a teacher able to make complex intelligence tools accessible while preserving the adult learner's agency, curiosity and chosen route towards expanded capability.

The trainee prepares a recovery step for a learner who loses the route. A visible example, saved state or short recap helps them re-enter the task. The observer checks whether that support preserves confidence and independent choice.

Source: S07 Intern Curriculum; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
