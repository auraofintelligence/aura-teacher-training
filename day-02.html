DAY 02 / MOVEMENT 01

A life map with room to grow

Build a small, recognisable life collection with meaningful links, review states, stable identities and chosen audiences.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Build a small, recognisable life collection with meaningful links, review states, stable identities and chosen audiences.

Categories organise the collection, while links explain why its contents belong together. An identifier lets a record remain recognisable when its title or location changes. Review states describe what has happened to the account: an unchecked summary and a person-reviewed declaration should remain distinguishable. An unvisited category is an available choice, not a demand to disclose.

A worked example

Illustrative example: Lee groups twelve sample records under making, relationships and future intentions. A recipe links to a shared meal and a wish to learn food photography. Lee changes the recipe record's title but keeps its identifier. The old wording remains in the revision note, and only an agreed recipe excerpt appears in the adult teaching demonstration.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose three categories and make twelve brief records. Include title, date where known, the person's wording, relevant context and source. Use fictional material for any category you prefer not to discuss.
  2. Add links across at least two categories. Label each relationship in ordinary language, such as learned with or useful for. Check whether another person can understand the link without seeing the entire collection.
  3. Mark selected records unchecked, declared, explored, revised or revisited as appropriate. Change one account and retain the prior version and reason. A spreadsheet or paper revision slip can demonstrate this process.
  4. Find one item through its title, category and a related memory. Then prepare an audience-limited teaching pack. Check the actual contents of that pack rather than assuming that a privacy label enforces access.

Teach it to another adult

Help an adult newcomer make three records using their own category names. Return the proposed wording for review and ask them to find one item through a relationship rather than its title.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A twelve-record collection with fields and cross-category links.
  • A revision showing stable identity, earlier wording and the reason for change.
  • Three retrieval routes and the exact records selected for teaching.

Try a changed condition

Rename a category and move one record to another view. Ask a peer to recover the same item and its history. Record any missing cue before changing the design.

Reflect on the result

  • Which links carry meaning beyond a category label?
  • Can unchecked content be recognised easily?
  • What information left the private collection for teaching?

Adapt the encounter

Use a dozen small cards, a narrated list or a familiar table. Let the learner choose category names and leave areas unvisited. Break entry and navigation into separate encounters if helpful.

Take it further

Compare a builder's field-based representation with a creative wall map. Identify the minimum shared identifiers and relationship descriptions needed to move between them without losing the learner's interpretation.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 2. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

The first organising skill is to give information a recognisable place. A life map links personal meaning with a structure that software also understands. Today's objective is a small, well-described collection that the learner enjoys revisiting. The current Aura Matrix Studio's categories and subcategories provide examples for a minimum viable self, while the learner supplies the content and additional categories that matter to them.

The learner chooses three broad categories such as family, creative expression and future intentions. Each category receives a few records with a title, the learner's words, a date, relevant context and a source where available. A family record might connect a recipe, a place and a particular person. A creative record might connect a song draft, its emotional purpose and a performance they remember. The useful unit is a meaningful relationship.

States help the person find their way. Unvisited identifies a category awaiting an encounter. Unchecked identifies content awaiting their review. Declared, explored, revised and revisited describe further work with a record. A record retains its previous versions and the reason for a change. A learner who changes a goal is able to see what the earlier intention meant in its own circumstances.

The studio task is to enter a dozen chosen records, connect at least two categories and then find a particular record using three routes: its title, its category and a related memory. Technical learners inspect the fields and stable identifiers. Other learners concentrate on whether the map feels recognisable and whether the interface helps them return. The group compares what makes a record useful to a human reader and to a personal model.

Public, private and secret selves introduce contextual permissions. The learner chooses an audience for a teaching example and keeps its underlying personal context within the scope they prefer. A public expression might be a song or skill description. A private record might be shared with a selected collaborator. A secret record retains its owner's chosen access. These decisions travel with the record when another interface displays it.

The reflection asks which areas of life received easy attention and which remain unchecked or unvisited. The learner identifies a reason without having to fill every category. That choice gives the next conversation direction and protects the usefulness of the starting map.

Teaching practice follows a newcomer making their first three records. The trainee offers categories, listens to the person's wording and returns the proposed map for review. Evidence consists of the small dataset, a navigation demonstration, the learner's corrections and a permissioned teaching example. The source connection is the movement from personal reflection to organised intelligence in AuraOZ and the current minimum viable self. [S04, introduction and Self-Reflection; S12, pp. 5-7, 22-23]
