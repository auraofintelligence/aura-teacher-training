DAY 50 / MOVEMENT 05

Habitats worth inhabiting

Design a habitat's daily life and dependencies together, then test how comfort, access and continuity change under pressure.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Design a habitat's daily life and dependencies together, then test how comfort, access and continuity change under pressure.

A habitat is simultaneously a physical system and a place people have reasons to inhabit. Food, waste, privacy, affection, culture and maintenance cannot be resolved independently. Keep concept layouts distinct from accepted engineering. In this studio, paper scenarios test relationships; they do not establish structural, pressure, life-support or emergency performance. Real access, evacuation and operating requirements need their relevant qualified authorities and configuration-specific review.

A worked example

Six fictional residents share a terrestrial analogue studio. A music room sits beside a quiet sleeping area and near the maintenance store. Increasing occupancy makes the noise conflict and storage access more consequential. Moving the room may help one resident but lengthen another's route. The design comparison now needs daily rhythms, mobility, privacy and maintenance evidence, alongside its illustrative resource budget.

Make, test and revise

  1. Write a mission and inhabitant brief using fictional adults with differing preferences. Identify duration, environment, privacy, contact and shared decision arrangements.
  2. Map a complete day through food, water, waste, power, learning, rest and recreation. Attach a responsible role to every recurring dependency.
  3. Rehearse increased occupancy, a limited resource and interrupted contact. Record how each change affects both services and the residents' experience of home.
  4. Let one resident propose a change for belonging or enjoyment. Compare two layouts and identify calculations or specialist reviews needed before any physical implementation.

Teach it to another adult

Lead another adult through one ordinary morning in the habitat. Ask them to change a preference and follow its effects across people and systems.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A habitat brief connecting mission, residents, daily rhythm and decision responsibilities.
  • A layout and dependency model with three labelled scenario traces and explicit assumptions.
  • A revision that balances belonging, access, privacy and maintenance without claiming engineering acceptance.

Try a changed condition

Move the concept from a terrestrial analogue to an off-world scenario. Identify assumptions that require new evidence rather than merely changing the scenery.

Reflect on the result

  • Whose ordinary day is hardest in this layout?
  • Which shared resource depends on human upkeep?
  • What would a real acceptance review still require?

Adapt the encounter

Use tactile room cards, a narrated day or a large-print layout. Let participants describe preferences through fictional residents without disclosing personal support needs.

Take it further

Prepare a handover for a new habitat steward, including unresolved resident choices, maintenance dependencies and the next test worth running.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 50. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

A habitat supports a community of living beings through physical systems, culture, privacy, care, learning and enjoyment. Today's objective is to design a small habitat for a chosen mission and test how it responds to changing conditions. The learner combines ecological, infrastructure, social and creative capabilities developed across the course.

The mission may be a terrestrial analogue, an underground community setting or an off-world design. The brief identifies inhabitants, purpose, duration, resources, environmental conditions, contact with the wider network and decision arrangements. A personal Aura helps each inhabitant describe what makes the setting worthwhile to live in.

The habitat studio creates a layout and dependency model. It locates living space, private areas, shared learning, food, water, waste, power, compute, care, maintenance and recreation. The learner follows a day's activities through the design. A beautiful room becomes part of a system with access, resources and people responsible for its upkeep.

The source alignment work explicitly places affection, culture, rest and the pleasure of shared activity in future habitats. Civilisation of Sand, Mineral Moonshots and the Straddie Space Station Simulator provide connected design contexts. The simulator's editable modules and scenario tools supply a practical starting point for a bounded exercise. [S12, pp. 19-20; Project Atlas: Civilisation of Sand, Mineral Moonshots, Straddie Space Station Simulator]

The class tests three changes: occupancy increases, a resource becomes limited and communication is interrupted. Agents model the dependencies and propose responses. Human participants review comfort, access, privacy, culture and shared responsibility. The record identifies which subsystem needs another calculation, experiment or specialist contribution.

The life reflection asks which conditions make a place feel like home. A learner may value quiet, music, familiar food, spiritual practice, companionship, freedom to create or the ability to help others. Those preferences enter the habitat alongside its physical requirements. Multiple best selves meet through a design that gives their lives room to develop.

Teaching practice leads a newcomer through the habitat's daily rhythm and asks them to change one condition. The trainee helps them trace the effect across people and systems. Evidence includes the mission brief, layout, resource dependencies, three scenarios, source and assumption notes, and teaching feedback. The learner now has a concrete bridge from everyday life to solar-system stewardship: design for beings who need reasons to flourish as well as systems that support their continued presence.

A resident role then proposes a change for enjoyment or belonging. The learner traces its resource implications and explores a workable design, showing how beauty, culture and practical support participate in the same habitat model.

Source: S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
