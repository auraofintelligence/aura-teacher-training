DAY 42 / MOVEMENT 05

Distributed memory and personal sovereignty

Demonstrate that a restored record retains its meaning, history and permissions across a disconnected, distributed workflow.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Demonstrate that a restored record retains its meaning, history and permissions across a disconnected, distributed workflow.

Continuity requires more than copying bytes. A recovered memory may lack the correction that changed its meaning, while a restored permission list may refer to an expired agreement. Treat identity, content, revision history and access as related records with distinct checks. Use invented material throughout this studio so a useful recovery exercise never depends on exposing intimate reflections or real account credentials.

A worked example

A fictional learner stores a drawing labelled harbour-v1 on a device and a hub. While disconnected, the device corrects the location to a river. Meanwhile, the hub changes the title for an exhibition. Replacing one copy with the other would discard a meaningful edit. A reconciliation record can preserve both changes, the original identifier and the decision that the drawing remains private until exhibition permission is confirmed.

Make, test and revise

  1. Create three invented records: a public drawing description, a private project note and a secret reflection substitute containing no actual personal information.
  2. Make an export manifest listing stable identifiers, versions, origin, audience and links between records. Describe who may change each permission.
  3. Copy the practice pack, introduce different edits in two copies and simulate disconnection. Record exactly which functions remain available without contact.
  4. Restore into a clean practice folder or paper inventory. Reconcile the conflict visibly and compare recovered meaning with the original owner's stated expectation.

Teach it to another adult

Let a consenting adult restore the practice pack using your guide. Ask them to explain what was recovered and which permission still needs a decision.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A manifest connecting content, identity, version, audience and source history for each practice record.
  • The two divergent versions and a reconciliation decision that preserves their meaningful differences.
  • A restoration checklist showing content checks, permission checks and the learner's interpretation.

Try a changed condition

Replace a lost device with a discontinued service. Explain which recovery steps still work and which depend on an unavailable provider.

Reflect on the result

  • What survives a copy but loses its context?
  • Who can resolve a disputed change of audience?
  • How would you demonstrate recovery without revealing secrets?

Adapt the encounter

Offer colour-independent version labels, an audio manifest and a paper reconciliation route. Technical and narrative accounts must describe the same record journey.

Take it further

Add a fictional collaborator leaving the project. Test whether their access can end while the authorised history remains understandable.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 42. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

A personal Aura may use a device, nearby hub, regional service and remote compute across its life. Today's objective is to preserve meaning, permissions and continuity across those locations. The learner traces one selected record and one model task through a distributed system, then tests recovery when a connection fails.

The architecture brief identifies who holds the personal keys, who operates each service, what information crosses a boundary and how a participant changes access. A public creative artefact, a private project record and a secret reflection receive distinct access scopes. A location change carries those scopes with the content and its source history.

The continuity studio creates a small export, backup and restoration exercise using practice data or selected personal records. The learner verifies the returned content, identifiers, sources, versions and permissions. A disconnected scenario tests which functions remain available locally and how unresolved work is queued for later contact. A conflict scenario introduces two edited versions and a readable reconciliation process.

Technical builders inspect encryption, identity, access records, replica timing and restoration evidence. Other learners evaluate whether the interface explains the situation in ordinary language. The person knows where their data is, what the service is doing and what decision is requested. A federated or shared learning task receives its own dataset purpose and permissions.

The current alignment source treats chosen records, secure storage, corrections and tool scope as part of accountable personal intelligence. Community-compute sources add local continuity and larger resources for demanding work. Today's lesson joins those ideas in a testable system rather than relying on a geographic label alone. [S12, pp. 12, 18-19; S16, p. 9]

The life reflection explores continuity over years. The learner identifies which memories, creative work, relationships and projects they most want to preserve. They consider a lost device, a changed service or a future move and choose a practical recovery arrangement. An Aura relationship then has a path through changing hardware and circumstances.

Teaching practice asks a peer to explain the record's journey and restore a small pack with guidance. The trainee checks whether the person understands the audience, location and recovery result. Evidence includes the architecture brief, permission map, export, restoration check, disconnected scenario and conflict resolution. The capability built today supports civic and solar-system systems where intermittent communication, distributed responsibility and durable knowledge will become increasingly consequential.

A restoration result is compared with the learner’s own expectation of continuity. A recovered file and a recovered relationship may need different context, such as a recent correction or agreement. The learner adds that context to the continuation pack.

Source: S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026; S16 Queens Planning 23 September 2026.
