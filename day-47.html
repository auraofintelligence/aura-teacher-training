DAY 47 / MOVEMENT 05

Resilience disaster rehearsal and mutual care

Rehearse a disruption with clear information handovers, human support dependencies and a reviewable recovery sequence.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Rehearse a disruption with clear information handovers, human support dependencies and a reviewable recovery sequence.

A tabletop rehearsal explores a plan's assumptions without becoming an emergency operation. Use an identified local planning source for context and label fictional changes clearly. Keep observations, reports and decisions distinct so participants can see why a choice was made. Include people maintaining the response: rest, relief, accessible communication and care affect whether a service can continue through the next stage.

A worked example

In a fictional community learning hub, a power outage interrupts a workshop while a bus connection is uncertain. The organiser has a source-dated transport page but no confirmed service update. Announcing that the bus is cancelled would invent a fact. A better rehearsal records what is known, who is checking the operator and how participants receive an update, including someone who cannot use the usual messaging channel.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose a paper scenario and identify its official planning context. Write the starting conditions, affected services and assumptions before assigning roles.
  2. Give each role a different information card. Rehearse a handover stating source, observation time, uncertainty, audience and the decision actually requested.
  3. Introduce an unavailable resource or additional access need. Trace the revised response through responsibility, communication and a route back to actual service authorities.
  4. Run a recovery discussion covering restored services, corrected records, relief for contributors and one change to test in the next rehearsal.

Teach it to another adult

Ask an adult newcomer to pass on a short situation report. Check whether they preserve uncertainty and distinguish a practice decision from official operational direction.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A labelled scenario and source pack with roles, dates and explicit exercise assumptions.
  • An information trace showing changed conditions, decisions and unresolved questions without invented updates.
  • A recovery plan including contributor support and a specific improvement for the next rehearsal.

Try a changed condition

Remove the usual communication channel. Demonstrate how the same essential handover reaches a participant through another agreed, accessible practice route.

Reflect on the result

  • Which statement was observed and which was assumed?
  • Who holds the relevant real operational authority?
  • What keeps the response team able to continue?

Adapt the encounter

Provide written and spoken scenario cards, predictable pauses and a quiet observer role. Do not simulate distress or require anyone to disclose disaster experiences.

Take it further

Repeat the exercise with a different recorder. Compare what a new team can reconstruct from the saved account before adding more complexity.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 47. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Resilience concerns how a community prepares, responds, recovers and learns when conditions change sharply. Today's objective is to rehearse a disruption in the community twin and identify the resources, roles and relationships that support people through it. The learner chooses a scenario relevant to the place and its actual planning sources.

The scenario may involve a power outage, interrupted transport, severe weather, water shortage or communication failure. The brief identifies the affected services, people with additional support needs, available resources and decision responsibilities. Official local arrangements and host knowledge provide the relevant context; the simulation labels its assumptions.

The resilience studio follows the event through time. The model changes the affected resources. Agents retrieve the selected guidance, identify dependencies and propose actions within their assigned scope. Human participants review the options from roles such as resident, carer, service steward, technical operator and coordinator. The three-observer method keeps the available information and personal experience legible.

AuraOZ includes a day on natural disasters, preparation and coordinated recovery. Big Picture Thinking connects skills transfer, civil uses for facilities and improved recovery. The current community-compute design adds local continuity when larger services are interrupted. Today's rehearsal develops those themes through an explicit scenario and handover. [S04, Day 11; S03, p. 8; S12, pp. 18-19]

The team tests a changed condition: a planned resource is unavailable or a participant needs another access route. The response shows who decides, how the information moves and which question remains unresolved. A recovery stage considers restoration, care, records and the lessons that will change the next plan.

The life reflection asks what helps the learner remain resourceful under uncertainty. Trusted people, clear information, a familiar task, a calm practice or an accessible tool may matter. They record the conditions that support their best self and choose a practical personal-preparation action relevant to their setting.

Teaching practice explains one dependency to a newcomer and asks them to rehearse an appropriate information handover. The trainee checks clarity, source timing and the person's understanding of their role. Evidence includes the scenario, source pack, service dependencies, role decisions, changed-condition response, recovery plan and feedback. The day's capability is compassionate systems judgement: technical continuity, local knowledge and care operate together, preparing the learner for wider planetary disruptions and future habitats.

The recovery discussion includes the people maintaining the response. The learner identifies rest, relief, communication and practical support for them, then adds those dependencies to the model. A resilient service also needs a workable human rhythm.

Source: S03 Big Picture Thinking; S04 AuraOZ Learning Progression and Thought Experiment 2017; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
