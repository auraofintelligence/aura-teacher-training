DAY 10 / MOVEMENT 01

Teaching through a meaningful encounter

Teach one personally meaningful Aura capability through a short adult encounter, independent practice and a revision informed by feedback.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Teach one personally meaningful Aura capability through a short adult encounter, independent practice and a revision informed by feedback.

Combine invitation, demonstration, learner action and a related attempt. Begin with the adult's interest and explain the relationship needed for their chosen action. The observer's account and learner's experience provide different evidence. Prepare a teaching pack that supports an independent return.

A worked example

Illustrative example: a newcomer wants to find notes for a community music session. The trainee could demonstrate making a source-linked record, let the newcomer create one for a chosen song and then ask them to locate a related equipment note. The observer would record where help was requested. No successful outcome is assumed before the encounter occurs.

Make, test and revise

  1. Choose one capability from days one to nine and a consenting adult peer. Write their starting interest, preferred format, intended action and evidence of understanding. Prepare sample information and a fifteen-minute lesson outline.
  2. Open with the person's question and a brief worked example. Invite them to perform the action while explaining their choices. Listen for meaning before offering the next cue, and leave room for repetition or a pause.
  3. Present a related example with one changed condition. Ask a second peer to observe within the agreed recording scope. Compare the learner's account, the resulting artefact and the sequence of help actually offered.
  4. Revise one confusing explanation or recovery step. Package the example, route, support options and way to resume. Invite the learner to choose whether to return, continue independently or ask another question.

Teach it to another adult

Ask the adult to explain the capability to an imagined newcomer using their own example. Notice whether their account includes the action's purpose, where to find its source and how to correct the result.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A lesson plan linked to the adult learner's chosen interest.
  • The learner's artefact, related-task attempt and agreed observer notes.
  • A revised reusable lesson with an independent return or recovery route.

Try a changed condition

Give the revised pack to another adult with different prior knowledge. Ask which first cue they need. Change the introduction around that need without adding the whole course as a prerequisite.

Reflect on the result

  • Which explanation connected with this person's life?
  • Where did you answer before understanding the difficulty?
  • What can the learner now do without you?

Adapt the encounter

Offer a screen, spoken demonstration, printed cards or a storyboard. Reduce the number of simultaneous tasks and let the adult choose pacing. Observation need not include audio, video or personal content.

Take it further

Prepare a technical version exposing fields and identifiers, and a creative or civic version using a meaningful story. Test both against the same action so changes in presentation do not hide a changed learning objective.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 10. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

Teaching begins with the learner in front of the teacher. An adult may arrive through a creative interest, a health question, a memory, a civic concern or curiosity about AI. Today's objective is to create a short encounter that connects such an interest to one useful Aura capability and leaves the person able to return independently.

The onboarding source offers a culture of enjoyment, inclusion, learning and asking for help. The volunteer curriculum adds explanation, practical exercises, mentorship, communication and feedback. The current alignment work recommends progressive discovery: explain one relationship before adding another component. These sources inform the course's teaching method. [S01, pp. 3-10; S07, pp. 3-11; S12, pp. 22-23]

The trainee chooses a capability from the first nine days: make a record, use a reflection lens, customise an avatar, follow a source, navigate a shell or activate a saved route. They prepare a fifteen-minute lesson for a chosen adult peer. The plan records what the person cares about, their preferred format, the action they will practise and the evidence that would show understanding.

The teaching studio begins with an invitation and a brief demonstration. The learner then performs the action while explaining their choices. The trainee listens for meaning and observes navigation, effort and points of confusion. They offer the next relevant cue, then give the learner space to repeat the task with a different example.

A second peer observes the encounter with agreed recording permissions. They note the sequence of explanations, questions, demonstrations and practice. The learner's own account sits beside that observation. A successful technical action and a meaningful experience each have evidence: the saved result, the learner's explanation and their ability to choose a next step.

The reflection examines the trainee's response to uncertainty. Did they add too much context? Did they rush to solve a problem before understanding it? Which explanation worked because it connected to the person's life? The trainee revises one part of the lesson and rehearses the change.

Technical builders create a reusable lesson pack containing sample data, interface route, support options and recovery steps. Creative teachers improve the story, imagery or spoken rhythm. Civic teachers connect the action to an ordinary community task. These contributions become shared teaching resources with their authorship and reuse terms attached.

Today's evidence includes the lesson plan, the learner's artefact, observation notes, feedback and the revised lesson. This first teaching checkpoint prepares the next movement of the course. Attention, memory, learning and reasoning will become deeper topics, while the same encounter-based method keeps them connected to the learner's own agency and expanding capability.

Source: S01 Aura Onboarding; S07 Intern Curriculum; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
