AURA / ADULT LEARNING AND STEWARDSHIP

Teaching practice

Plan a meaningful encounter, observe without taking over and revise through feedback.

In this guide

Plan around the adult in front of you

Ask about the person’s interest, experience, preferred format and useful next action. Agree a narrow objective before introducing the wider system. Day 10 develops a fifteen-minute encounter; Day 39 revisits access across different conditions, and Day 58 develops a substantial practicum.

  1. Invite the learner’s question in their own words.
  2. Choose one action and a visible result.
  3. Prepare a familiar example and a changed example.
  4. Agree what an observer may record.
  5. Keep a recovery route and an unhurried stopping point.

Demonstrate, then make room

Explain why the action relates to the learner’s interest. Demonstrate it briefly and let them attempt it. Listen for the reason behind a choice. Offer the smallest useful cue instead of completing the task for them. Repeating the demonstrated clicks is an early observation; using the idea with different material asks a deeper question.

An illustrative lesson might connect a chosen photograph with a story, its source and the person’s correction. For transfer, the learner chooses a different photograph and explains what context they need before interpreting it.

Keep different evidence distinct

  • The artefact shows what was saved or made.
  • The learner’s account explains what the encounter meant to them.
  • An observer records a specific action or difficulty within the agreed scope.
  • A changed-condition task shows what carried over and what needed support.
  • A correction identifies how the lesson or system should change.

Change the conditions without losing the concept

A learner can explain a relationship aloud instead of writing it, use a tactile model instead of an immersive view, or practise in shorter sessions. Ask what helps; do not infer a person’s needs from a diagnostic label. Check whether the resulting evidence still addresses the intended concept.

These are adult learning methods. Activity-specific competence, supervision, professional duties and child-safe requirements are separate from this course.

Review the teacher as well as the learner

Ask where the explanation connected with the person’s life, where it added unnecessary detail and what the teacher did when uncertain. Include unsuccessful attempts and feedback from someone who chose to stop. Revise one part, rehearse it and keep the reason for the change.
