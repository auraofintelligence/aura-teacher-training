DAY 14 / MOVEMENT 02

A training collection that preserves the person

Prepare an example collection with source and permission context, keeping learning examples separate from reserved evaluation material.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Prepare an example collection with source and permission context, keeping learning examples separate from reserved evaluation material.

Begin with a desired capability, then show context, useful responses and why they fit. The dataset card records provenance, audience, gaps and revision. Preparing examples does not train a model; human review and a reserved test set can complete this exercise.

A worked example

Illustrative example: a fictional learner wants summaries of workshop planning notes to retain dates, unresolved questions and the next responsible person. Six sample exchanges could demonstrate the desired structure. Two different notes could be held back for evaluation, including one whose date is uncertain. Repeating a training example would test recall of that example rather than the intended changed-condition performance.

Make, test and revise

  1. State one narrow capability and write a dataset card covering sources, audience, permission scope, example types, known gaps and corrections. Choose sample or personally reviewed material, keeping another person's contributions outside the collection unless their use is agreed.
  2. Draft several question-context-response examples and explain why each response fits. Include different circumstances and one changed interpretation with its date. Keep source wording distinguishable from the desired response and the learner's judgement about it.
  3. Review for duplicates, contradictory labels, missing conditions and lost meaning. Set aside a separate evaluation subset before making revisions that depend on its answers. Record the split and which material a hypothetical training process would receive.
  4. Ask a second reviewer to inspect the collection's meaning and one reserved challenge. Resolve an ambiguity with the learner, then version the examples and correction log. If no training is performed, report readiness and open questions rather than model improvement.

Teach it to another adult

Help an adult peer prepare three examples for their own narrow purpose. Ask them to distinguish source, interpretation and desired response, then explain why one fresh question should remain outside the learning examples.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • A dataset card with purpose, provenance, audience and known limitations.
  • Reviewed examples, a documented split and reserved evaluation questions.
  • A second review and correction log preserving the learner's intended meaning.

Try a changed condition

Use a reserved example with a changed responsibility or uncertain fact. Can a reviewer distinguish a context-sensitive response from a familiar phrase, and explain what further evidence a model comparison would require?

Reflect on the result

  • What specific capability do these examples demonstrate?
  • Which condition would be lost in a simplified label?
  • Has evaluation material influenced the learning set?

Adapt the encounter

Use a small card collection rather than code or a large dataset. Permit fictional examples and spoken review. The person can exclude sensitive material; completeness of personal disclosure is not the lesson objective.

Take it further

A builder can draft a portable example schema and duplicate report. A creative or civic participant can add contrasting contexts that reveal whether the desired structure preserves humour, uncertainty or responsibility accurately.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 14. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

A training collection is a designed body of examples. Today's objective is to prepare selected personal material so a model learns useful relationships and the learner is able to evaluate the result. The work begins with the person's intended capability, chosen sources and permissions, then separates examples for learning from examples reserved for later assessment.

The learner selects a narrow first purpose: summarise a particular kind of reflection, respond in their preferred language, retrieve a project history or help plan a familiar activity. They write examples of the desired exchange. Each example includes the question, relevant context, a useful response and any explanation of why that response fits.

The studio task creates a small dataset card. It describes the purpose, source records, audience, permission scope, example types, known gaps and revision process. Personal material receives its public, private or secret scope. Contributions from another person require that person's agreed use. The learner reviews the wording before the collection is handed to a training process.

Technical builders examine formatting, duplicated examples, inconsistent labels and source links. They prepare a training subset and a separate evaluation subset. The learner checks the human meaning of each item. A deliberately difficult evaluation example asks the model to handle a changed condition, such as a goal constrained by a new responsibility, rather than repeat a familiar phrase.

The source agent paper makes discovery with the human part of function development, and the current alignment paper makes selected material the foundation of a personal intelligence. This lesson applies the same relationship to training data: understand the capability sought, prepare the examples, test the result and return corrections to the collection. [S09, pp. 20-24; S12, pp. 7, 12]

The life reflection looks for variety. A person may be thoughtful in one setting, playful in another and uncertain while learning. The collection includes context for those differences. The learner chooses an example where their present understanding has changed and retains the earlier account with its date and explanation. This protects continuity while allowing development.

Teaching practice asks a peer to build three examples for their own chosen purpose. The trainee helps them distinguish a source, an interpretation and a desired response, then checks the proposed audience. Evidence includes the dataset card, a reviewed example set, reserved evaluation questions and a correction log. The result is a compact, inspectable foundation for personal-model development and later comparisons between model versions.

A second reviewer checks whether each example conveys the person’s intended meaning. Their questions identify an ambiguous phrase or missing condition, which the learner resolves before adding the example to the reviewed collection.

Source: S09 Mixture of Experts Function Callers and AutoGen Agents; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
