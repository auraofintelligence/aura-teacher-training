DAY 24 / MOVEMENT 03

Specialists with complementary capabilities

Create complementary specialist roles whose distinct contributions can be traced into one shared result.

In this guide

PurposeWorked exampleStudio stepsTeaching and evidenceReview and accessOriginal readingNext stepPractical resources ↗

Expanded studio guide · 28 October 2026

What you will practise

Create complementary specialist roles whose distinct contributions can be traced into one shared result.

A specialist role needs an actual job, relevant context and a defined handover. Calling three general responses researcher, designer and critic does not establish complementary expertise. The source’s mastermind and agent proposals invite support and challenge around a shared purpose; today tests that arrangement through a modest artefact. Keep human accountability explicit: a named learner owns the brief and approves the final draft. A role may be enacted by people, separate prompts or configured demonstration agents. Record which arrangement was used, since a classroom role-play does not demonstrate deployed multiagent infrastructure.

A worked example

Illustrative example: the team drafts an accessible audio-walk introduction for a fictional community garden. The researcher returns a source table and open questions. The designer turns supported facts into a short route and script. The critic checks the script against the source table and access brief. None may infer permission to record residents or publish the walk; the human owner retains that decision.

Make, test and revise

  1. Write three role cards with purpose, selected inputs, expected output, allowed functions, information boundaries and a handover recipient. Give each role a different acceptance question.
  2. Rehearse a broad-instruction baseline in which every role receives the same vague request. Preserve duplicated work, unsupported statements and gaps as observations rather than correcting them silently.
  3. Repeat with the defined cards. Trace one fact from the researcher’s source through the designer’s script to the critic’s check, retaining the source identifier at every handover.
  4. Exchange designer and critic roles. Supply the context each newly assigned role needs, then compare whether the shared purpose survives and where responsibilities still overlap.

Teach it to another adult

Ask a newcomer which role they would approach for a missing source, an awkward explanation or a publication decision. Improve the cards if responsibility cannot be located.

Keep evidence someone can inspect

  • Three role cards with distinct outputs and permission boundaries.
  • Baseline and structured traces showing duplication, gaps and usable handovers.
  • One source-to-script-to-review chain and a documented role-exchange finding.

Try a changed condition

Move the team to a materials-experiment brief. The peer must redefine subject-specific evidence and explain which human expertise is required before any physical experiment could occur.

Reflect on the result

  • What evidence shows that the specialist contributed a different capability?
  • Which context was lost between two roles?
  • Who remains answerable when the final draft contains an error?

Adapt the encounter

Use role badges, plain-language cards and one shared task board. Permit oral contributions or annotated examples while keeping attribution and handover fields consistent.

Take it further

Add a teacher role only after identifying a missing teaching output. Compare its contribution with simply asking the designer to write more, and retain the arrangement that serves the brief.

The original daily reading

Preserved reading from the 14 October 2026 course, PDF page 24. The studio guide above adds practical detail; it does not replace this source.

A specialist agent performs a defined role using selected knowledge, tools and permissions. Today's objective is to assemble a small team whose contributions complement one another. The learner begins with a shared question, then assigns responsibilities according to the work required and the evidence each role will return.

The project may involve a learning experience, a material experiment, a habitat scene or a civic source pack. A researcher retrieves relevant material. A designer proposes a representation. A critic examines assumptions and tests. A teacher shapes the encounter for a newcomer. A coordinator connects the results. The learner gives each role enough context to serve the same purpose.

The studio task creates three agent cards. Each card describes the role, inputs, outputs, data scope, available functions, review conditions and handover. Technical builders configure the agents in a practice system. Other learners review the role definitions and provide examples of good work. The team completes a small task with a shared result and an event history.

The agent source proposes task, supervisor and communication roles, feedback and revision requests. It also explores expert teams across health, learning, creative media, smart environments, longevity, materials and environmental response. The course selects roles relevant to the learner's project and makes their actual capability visible through the work they produce. [S09, pp. 16-24]

The class compares two team arrangements. In one, all agents receive the same broad instruction. In the other, each receives a defined contribution and a clear exchange with the next role. The learner examines duplication, missing context, completion quality and the clarity of the resulting handover. The comparison identifies which structure supports useful cooperation.

The life reflection looks at the learner's human circles. Friends, teachers, craftspeople, carers and researchers bring different strengths. The learner identifies where complementary expertise helped them and where unclear responsibilities caused difficulty. They apply that insight to the agent cards and to a real collaboration they are developing.

Teaching practice introduces a peer to the team through the shared question. The trainee explains what each agent receives, what it returns and how the result is reviewed. The peer identifies which role they would ask for help in a changed scenario. Evidence includes the cards, two team traces, compared outcomes and teaching feedback. Today's work develops the mastermind principle into a concrete multiagent setting: distinct capabilities, a common purpose and usable connections among results. [S06, pp. 2-4; S12, pp. 23-24]

A role exchange lets the designer review and the critic propose. The learner observes whether the same purpose survives the change and which additional context each role needs, creating a useful test of collaborative adaptability.

Source: S06 Mastermind Swarmwise; S09 Mixture of Experts Function Callers and AutoGen Agents; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
